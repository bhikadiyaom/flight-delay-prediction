## Key facts and numbers (all measured, copied from the artifacts)

- Data: 6,830,352 flights after cleaning; 86,991 cancelled; 1,386,688 delayed (arrival delay >= 15 min).
- Split: train Jan-Aug (4.46M), validate/calibrate September, test Oct-Dec (1.73M).
- Production delay model (XGBoost): **ROC AUC 0.661**, F1 0.330, precision 0.238, recall 0.534; **Brier 0.124 after calibration (0.201 before)**.
- Cancel model: AUC 0.706. Delay-minutes model: MAE 35.9 min vs 36.5 for the median baseline.
- Random split (not deployable) AUC 0.745: the gap to 0.661 is the leakage a random split hides.
- Four model families within 0.015 AUC of each other: the limit is the information in the data.

## Suggested honest framing for the results chapter

1. Predicting delays from schedule, airport, airline history and daily weather is genuinely hard; the model ranks flights better than chance (AUC 0.66) and, more usefully, produces **calibrated probabilities** with **explanations**.
2. The evaluation is deliberately strict (future months, no leakage), so numbers are lower than a random split would give, and this is reported, not hidden.
3. A dataset-level discovery: aircraft columns leak the cancellation label (they are empty exactly for cancelled flights) and had to be excluded.

## Future work (already identified)

Hourly weather and exact scheduled time; the previous leg of the same aircraft (late-arriving aircraft is 40% of delay minutes); daily refresh of the rolling history;
retraining on a full year; per-airline/airport models; online monitoring of calibration drift using `PredictionLog.actual`.
